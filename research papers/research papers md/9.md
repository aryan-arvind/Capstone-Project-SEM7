# **1. Introduction** 

As AI rapidly evolves, Multi-Agent Systems (MAS) demonstrate wide opportunities in autonomous driving, UAV swarms and so on, and multi-agent collaborative decision-making plays the central role in complex tasks. Nevertheless, the current models have disadvantages on costliness of computation/communication as well as poor scaling, particularly in resource-limited edge platforms. The classical centralized training strategies achieve global optimization at the expense of increasing exponentially the scale of parameters and computational load with a larger number of agents, and global information sharing frequently accelerates bandwidth congestion and makes the practical use of such strategies unfeasible. 

To address them, the given paper suggests a lightweight multi-agent collaborative decision-making model of digital AI, with a novel hierarchical architecture and dynamic scheduling. The three-levels model contains lightweight node tier, an edge coordination tier and cloud strategy tier. A design of event-driven scheduling strategy is tailored to invoke updates to state errors, and perception-interaction-consensus-execution closed loop reduces communication resources through VAEs, attention mechanisms and virtual game playing of Nash equilibrium. Theoretically, a mathematical model Dec-POMDP model is constructed with an extensive utility function to trade task completion and resource usage and with the Lyapunov criterion of stability, the system errors lapse exponentially. 

The key contributions of this paper are four-fold: (1) The three-tier architecture reduces parameters to 1/5 of the methods of conventional approaches, through knowledge distillation and eliminates 82 percent of the computation to be deployed as an edge. (2) 35% saving of computing resources compared to fixed-period techniques demonstrates that event-based scheduling can be as efficient as experimented on-demand scheduling. (3) Sparse communication mechanism in terms of attention cuts almost 80 perception of communication overheads that are helpful in sustaining big scale MAS in terms of real time collaboration. (4) A full theoretical framework is developed, and Lyapunov method proves that the system is stable, which has theoretical justification of the dynamic scheduling. 

# **2. Related Work** 

Multi-Agent Systems (MAS) constitute a core research direction in the field of artificial intelligence. With the key characteristics of distributed autonomous collaboration and multi-node distributed perception and decisionmaking, they show remarkable practical value in complex scenarios such as autonomous driving platooning, UAV swarms, collaborative control of the Industrial Internet of Things, and edge intelligent networking. Among them, collaborative decision-making technology is the core support for systems to accomplish complex tasks and achieve – global optimality [1 3]. In recent years, scholars worldwide have conducted extensive research on four core aspects of multi-agent collaborative decision-making: architecture design, model optimization, communication mechanisms, and theoretical modeling. However, intractable bottlenecks still exist in key issues including deployment on resource-constrained edge platforms, expansion of large-scale agent clusters, and balanced scheduling of computing and communication resources. Based on the core research context of this field, the following section reviews the progress and existing shortcomings of related work. 

Traditional multi-agent collaborative decision-making is mainly divided into two mainstream architectures: centralized and distributed. These two architectures differ significantly in global optimization performance, resource consumption, and scalability, forming the fundamental research thread of the field [4]. The centralized architecture was the mainstream scheme in the early stage, which relies on a central control node to complete global information collection, policy training, and instruction issuance. It can theoretically achieve globally optimal decision-making in small-scale and fixed scenarios. Nevertheless, as the number of agents increases, the computational load and communication pressure on the central node surge exponentially. Meanwhile, it suffers from single point of failure and extremely poor system robustness, making it completely unsuitable for resource-constrained platforms such as – edge terminals and embedded devices [5 6]. The distributed architecture emerged to overcome the bottlenecks of the centralized architecture. Each agent independently performs perception and decision-making based on local information and realizes collaboration only through local interaction, which greatly reduces the single-node computing pressure and global communication overhead, and significantly improves scalability and robustness. However, restricted by local information perception, this architecture is prone to task conflicts and insufficient

global decision-making consistency. Even though some improved schemes optimize the local collaboration logic, they still fail to solve the problems of communication redundancy and resource waste in large-scale scenarios, and – can hardly balance task performance and resource consumption [7 8]. 

To address the pain points of complex computation and excessive parameters in multi-agent models, the academic community has carried out extensive research on lightweight optimization, focusing on three core directions: model compression, knowledge distillation, and lightweight network design. However, most existing achievements concentrate on optimization for single-agent scenarios, while dedicated modifications for multi-agent collaborative – scenarios are relatively scarce [9 10]. Existing lightweight technologies can effectively reduce the parameter volume and computational cost in single-agent perception and reasoning tasks. Yet when directly migrated to multi-agent collaborative scenarios, they often neglect the interactive and collaborative characteristics among agents, leading to a notable drop in decision-making accuracy after lightweight processing, and fail to balance lightness and decisionmaking effectiveness [11]. 

Excessive communication overhead is the core factor restricting the practical implementation of large-scale multiagent collaborative decision-making. Current research mainly focuses on three approaches: fixed-period communication, sparse communication, and attention mechanism-based interaction, aiming to reduce communication redundancy and improve resource utilization [12]. Fixed-period communication is the most basic interaction scheme with simple implementation logic, but it synchronizes information periodically regardless of changes in agent states. In large-scale cluster scenarios, this will quickly exhaust bandwidth resources, resulting in prominent resource waste [13].

# _3.2. Core Mechanism of Hierarchical Dynamic System Scheduling_ 

The model is based upon a three-level hierarchical design of the lightweight computing and effective teamwork. The lightweight node layer, which is equipped with pruned lightweight neural networks and compressed sensing modules, performs local perception of agents and real-time response and manages the single-agent memory signature as a preset value. The edge coordination level clears up the conflicts at local groups and allows quick decision modification through local consensus algorithm to collaboration in the short term. The cloud strategy tier does the work of global task planning whereby lower tiers are guided by periodically issuing simplified policy rules to prevent real time channeling of large scale model parameters. This hierarchical architecture reduces the communication bandwidth requirement of the system, provides dynamically robustness to the environment, and knowledge distillation further improves lightweight execution, removing compact student models of complex teacher ones.

# _3.3. Implementation Process of Multi-Agent Collaborative Decision-Making_ 

Multi-agent collaborative decision-making is based on perception-interaction -consensus- execution closed loop. In perception, dimensionality reduction and compression of the environmental features of each agent is carried out using a VAE in order to reduce the amount of data transmission. When it comes to communication, attention-based mechanism provides agents with an opportunity to share only the important state information, preventing the broadcast storm, and keeping the system application lightweight. The game-theoretic Fictitious Play algorithm is used in consensus: the agents locally mimic the behavior of other agents and solve the Nash equilibrium of the conflict by iteratively simulating other players and solving the Nash equilibrium, becoming efficient in 10 iterations. During execution, the outcome of the decisions is transmitted to the node tier; the system will transition to the highfrequency sampling to do dynamic replanning in the system upon the detection of environmental mutations. This loop guarantees real-time collaboration in uncertain environments and minimizes communication overhead via dimensionality reduction and sparse communication.
