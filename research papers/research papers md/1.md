Abstract: This paper proposes a multi-agent system centered on large language models to address the issues that traditional well log interpretation relies on expert experience and poses great difficulty in multi-disciplinary collaboration and constructs a digital twin architecture across three dimensions of agents, tools and environment. At the agent level, a role-based architecture is established to decompose the complex log interpretation workflow into independent subtasks, enabling structured transfer of expert knowledge. At the tool level, petrophysical formulas and machine learning algorithms are encapsulated to form a phys- ics-data dual-path hybrid reasoning mechanism; at the environment level, a standardized digital twin space is established based on the Model Context Protocol to achieve closed-loop control of the entire workflow. Engineers can drive the system through natural language commands to complete the full log interpretation process from data loading and parameter calcula- tion to reservoir classification, realizing end-to-end automation from raw data to interpretation conclusions. In tests on 100 field wells, the system generates key interpretation parameters that are highly consistent with expert results, exhibiting stable recognition capability for complex reservoir types. This study demonstrates that this human-machine collaborative working mode significantly enhances the standardization and efficiency of well log interpretation, providing technical reference for in- telligent transformation of highly specialized industrial processes.

## Introduction

Oil and gas exploration and development is critical to ensuring national energy security, involving multidisci- plinary collaboration across geology, geophysical pros- pecting, reservoir engineering, and computer science [1–4]. Well logging interpretation bridges geological exploration and engineering decision-making, encompassing both standardized computational workflows and a strong re- liance on expert empirical judgment, making it a typical scenario that exemplifies this mode of interdisciplinary collaboration [5–6]. In recent years, large language models (LLMs) have demonstrated emergent capabilities in

For data management, retrieval-augmented generation technology oriented toward subsurface domains has enabled auto- mated parsing and intelligent retrieval of unstructured data such as production logs and vertical seismic pro- files [21]. In terms of engineering decision-making, multi- agent systems have begun to be applied in areas such as well log curve matching [22], legacy well curve reconstruc- tion and potential zone identification [23], full-lifecycle downhole maintenance [24], and autonomous optimization of wellbore operation strategies [25]. However, current multi-agent architectures have yet to penetrate the core computational and reasoning workflows of well logging interpretation. Current software remains heavily de- pendent on expert experience for layer-by-layer parame- ter coordination and anomaly identification, lacking the capacity for continuous geological context reasoning and adaptive optimization, and thus struggling to meet the demands of high-frequency iterative engineering workflows.

To address the above issues, we developed a multi-agent collaborative system driven by large language models. At the agent level, the system is designed following the team structure and role division of field engineering teams, with the standard well logging interpretation workflow as the main thread for task decomposition and coordina- tion. At the tool level, physical formulas for shale content, porosity, permeability, and other parameters are inte- grated with data-driven methods to construct a phys- ics-data dual-driven hybrid reasoning pathway, balancing result interpretability and accuracy. At the environment level, existing well logging interpretation tools and data processing scripts are encapsulated as standard inter- faces, allowing agents to invoke professional tools for computational tasks without modifying the existing production system. Engineers can intervene at key deci- sion nodes via natural language, grounding the large model's task planning capability in concrete engineering computations. This work aims to address practical chal- lenges in well logging interpretation, specifically the high dependence on expert experience and the difficulty of multi-professional collaboration. This is achieved by automatically coordinating professional tools, intelli- gently selecting parameters, and automatically reviewing results, thereby transitioning from tool-driven to intelli- gent decision-making and improving the efficiency and decision quality of complex reservoir evaluation.

Test results based on 100 field wells show that the sys- tem can independently complete the full well logging

interpretation workflow, with reservoir classification results broadly consistent with expert interpretations, and demonstrate the capability to extend from parameter computation to comprehensive evaluation. The system can dynamically optimize formula parameters based on geological understanding and real-time data, and gener- ate traceable reasoning records, making full use of the contextual understanding capability of large language models. Through a role-based architecture and inter- pretable reasoning chains, the system structures expert experience and makes workflows transparent, providing a feasible pathway for oil and gas exploration to transi- tion from experience-guided manual operation to knowledge-guided intelligent operation.

## 1. Multi-agent collaborative architecture for well log interpretation

In the system, each agent takes a large language model as its reasoning core and integrates perception, reasoning, action, and memory modules, thereby transforming the base language model into an agent system capable of autonomous decision-making and task execution in a controlled environment. The perception module is re- sponsible for collecting and structurally compressing environmental information. The reasoning module de- composes task objectives into executable plans based on semantic understanding. The action module performs specific computations by invoking predefined tools. The memory module stores task history and domain knowl- edge, providing contextual support for continuous rea- soning. The four modules are connected through stan- dardized interfaces, forming a closed loop from envi- ronmental perception to action feedback, and ensuring safe and controllable execution under a permis- sion-isolation mechanism, ultimately constructing an intelligent well logging interpretation system with both autonomy and reliability.

## 1.1. Core components of the agent

The perception module acquires information from the external environment and converts unstructured data into text summaries processable by the large language model. This module obtains the locations of data files, scripts, and reports through environment scanning tools, and retrieves statistical summaries and key features us- ing data analysis tools. This design both reduces the im- pact of context length limitations of the large language model and aligns with expert analysis habits.

The reasoning module serves as the decision-making core of the agent, receiving environmental information and task instructions from the perception module, and generating structured action plans after analysis. These plans are defined in JSON (JavaScript Object Notation) format, explicitly specifying the tools to be invoked, the


parameters to be passed, and the execution sequence, thereby transforming engineer intent into instruction sequences that are machine-understandable and ma- chine-executable.

The action module is responsible for translating the decisions of the reasoning module into actual operations, parsing instructions from the plan and invoking prede- fined tools to complete data processing and computation. This module constitutes the sole interface through which the agent interacts with the working environment, en- suring the safety and traceability of all operations.

The memory module stores both long-term and short- term memory. Long-term memory is preserved in the form of structured logs, forming complete and traceable task records. Short-term memory is derived from docu- ment retrieval augmentation and recent interaction his- tory, including inter-agent communications, tool invoca- tion records, and user instructions, providing the neces- sary contextual support for coherent task execution.

## 2. Operational logic of the multi-agent system

Fig. 3 illustrates the operational logic of the agents. Upon program initialization, a virtual team comprising multiple domain expert agents is instantiated, with each agent's role, responsibilities, and capability scope defined via prompts, and dedicated knowledge bases loaded ac- cording to task requirements. During the execution phase, agents operate through a closed-loop "observation–ac- tion–feedback" cycle: acquiring state information from the environment, invoking tools to complete well logging interpretation steps, and adjusting subsequent actions based on feedback. The environment encompasses three primary elements: raw well logging data and intermedi- ate results; an agent interaction architecture grounded in standard well logging interpretation workflows; and a toolkit developed for well logging interpretation, covering both theoretical formula computation and machine learning inference modules. The tool layer serves as the engineered extension of agent capabilities and constitutes the sole outlet for numerical outputs. Agents drive tool execution through control files, retaining the authority to observe computational results but not to directly modify numerical values. This mechanism ensures that all out- puts are processed through verifiable physical or mathematical tools, thereby suppressing the influence of model hallucinations on processing results.

After the engineer sets objectives in natural language, the chief engineer agent receives the instructions, parses the task intent, and decomposes the workflow, specifying the domain expert agents to be invoked and their collabora- tion sequence. The agents invoke function tools to ana- lyze the curve coverage, well count, and sample size of the raw logging data required for the task. ## 3.2. Well logging interpretation task evaluation

## 3.2.1. Experimental dataset and evaluation criteria

The well logging interpretation results are empirically evaluated using an industrial-grade dataset comprising 100 wells of field logging data. Each well contains multi- ple types of sensor response curves, including lithology, physical properties, and electrical properties. The core task of the agent system is to transform raw logging data into two categories of geological information: obtaining

key petrophysical interpretation parameters (shale con- tent, porosity, permeability, and water saturation); and performing qualitative reservoir classification.

In actual operations, well logging data frequently suffer from missing data problems due to cost control and varying requirements at different exploration stages. In addition, missing values and outliers caused by physical measurement limitations or interference during data acquisition also occur within individual curves. This leads to an exponential increase in the combinatorial complexity of interpretation tasks. Traditional ap- proaches rely on expert empirical judgment, requiring substantial human effort. The agent-based well logging interpretation system, by contrast, needs to assess the current state of the data and autonomously search for valid interpretation paths under the constraints of do- main knowledge and optimization objectives, achieving the transition from experience-driven to systematized intelligent reasoning.

The data-driven models within the system are trained on a reserved subset of well data and calibrated using a small amount of high-precision core analysis data. Dur- ing the evaluation phase, the system takes as input well data that were not involved in training and completes the full workflow from data diagnosis to final interpretation. The experiments use expert field results as the reference, employing accuracy to evaluate the computational preci- sion of key interpretation parameters, and comprehen- sively assessing reservoir classification conclusions using accuracy, recall, and F1 score.

## 3.2.3. Calculation performance of interpretation parameters

The theoretical formula method is grounded in petrophysical theory and well logging engineering formulas, offering clear physical meaning and strong interpretability, and is capable of providing robust geological inferences even in new blocks lacking prior data. The data-driven module integrates various machine learning methods, including XGBoost, MLP, LSTM, and Transformers. It leverages their feature extraction and nonlinear fitting capabilities to mine complex mapping relationships between well logging responses and reservoir parameters derived from his- torical data. Additionally, it accurately learns the local- ized empirical interpretation embedded in engineers' processing workflows for specific blocks.

This simplification based on limited information often leads to systematic deviations between the calculated results and the actual reservoir characteristics. ## 4. Conclusions

Following the digital mapping logic from the real world to the twin world, the intelligent well logging interpreta- tion system is constructed across three dimensions, namely agent roles, professional tools, and operational


environment, forming an engineering agent-based para- digm that is practically deployable.

The agent role design establishes a position-based ar- chitecture grounded in large language models, decom- posing global planning and professional analysis respon- sibilities among the chief engineer agent and domain expert agents for shale content, porosity, and other pa- rameters. By explicitly defining capability boundaries and specializing toolsets, the complexity of long planning is reduced. This hierarchical collaboration, which simulates real-world expert teams, achieves the structured inheri- tance of expert experience through the explicit binding of position responsibilities and knowledge bases.

The professional tool layer transforms well logging domain knowledge into callable modules by encapsulat- ing a library of petrophysical theoretical formulas, thereby enhancing engineering interpretability. Mean- while, leveraging the reasoning capabilities of large lan- guage models, petrophysical theoretical formulas and machine learning algorithms are jointly encapsulated as structured operational modules, establishing a phys- ics-data dual-path decision mechanism. This enables the system to automatically switch to physical formulas when data are scarce to ensure system stability, and to invoke machine learning models when data are sufficient to ap- proach expert-level processing, achieving adaptive deci- sion-making under complex data conditions. In addition, the system adopts an isolation design between configura- tion-driven and tool-execution layers, prohibiting large models from directly generating numerical results, thereby suppressing the risk of model hallucination.

The operational environment establishes a standard- ized runtime space based on the Model Context Protocol (MCP). Drawing on standard well logging interpretation workflows, this environment establishes an operational procedure of data collection, data quality inspection, data preprocessing, and interpretation evaluation, ensuring that multiple agents effectively leverage industrial prac- tice experience for task orchestration and intermediate result handoff. The digital twin environment relies on structured log analysis to form traceable decision records, enabling full-process controllability from data input to interpretation conclusions.

Experiments conducted on 100 wells demonstrate that the system is capable of autonomously constructing and executing complete well logging interpretation workflows, with final interpretation results highly consistent with expert conclusions. This outcome validates the potential of multi-agent collaborative architectures in addressing real-world engineering problems.

Although the intelligent well logging interpretation system has demonstrated its effectiveness, the current implementation still has several limitations. Regarding tools, although the system integrates existing well logging

interpretation tools and provides standardized extension interfaces, its operation is strictly confined to a prede- fined toolset. As large models evolve toward embodied intelligence, agents will need the capability to continu- ously learn from the environment and solve new prob- lems. However, the constraints of the predefined toolset prevent agents from independently writing or debugging new scripts to handle unexpected issues. Therefore, de- veloping secure sandbox environments for agents to autonomously write, debug, and execute codes would enhance their flexibility. Regarding agents, this work validates the effectiveness of the divide–conquer archi- tecture, which ensures system stability by drawing on organizational experience and incorporating standard well logging workflows. However, the agent interaction structure still relies on manual presets, limiting its adap- tive adjustment capability when confronted with new scenarios. Future work should explore structure optimi- zation mechanisms based on environmental feedback to achieve autonomous optimization of agent collaboration structures. Regarding the environment, although the system constructs a twin environment that aggregates data and logs, the influx of information as the scale of information and the number of tools grow will lead to increased processing noise. How to optimize agent memory mechanisms and interaction efficiency while ensuring context integrity, and suppress interference from irrelevant information, is an important research direction for improving system scalability.

In summary, this system constructs a practically de- ployable human-AI collaborative solution for well logging interpretation through the synergistic design of agent roles, professional tools, and operational environment across three levels. Despite current limitations in tool autonomous evolution, agent dynamic topology, and en- vironmental information denoising, the framework has validated its engineering feasibility in large-scale testing across 100 wells. By replacing the domain knowledge base and expert agents, the architecture can be extended to other geoscience problems such as seismic interpreta- tion and reservoir numerical simulation, providing a ref- erence for the intelligent upgrading of highly specialized industrial workflows. This work validates the core value of human-AI collaboration: experts are responsible for top-level geological logic and critical decision supervision based on intuition and experience, while AI systems un- dertake knowledge integration and computational execu- tion through standardized protocols, with the two com- plementing each other. As the capabilities of underlying models continue to improve, agent systems of this kind, characterized by human-in-the-loop supervision and structured design as the core, are expected to play an increasingly important role in a broader range of complex engineering tasks.
